Space4Ag's 2026 Hosts

We would like to thank our incredible hosts for opening their homes and businesses to us,
and for sharing their invaluable knowledge on this tour.

Texas

Billy Tiller

Bus Host & Emcee, Monday Morning · Founder, Grower Information Services Cooperative

Billy Tiller is a fourth-generation farmer in Lamb and Bailey Counties, about an hour northwest of Lubbock, and the founder of Grower Information Services Cooperative (GiSC) — the first cooperative formed to represent growers' interests on the question of who owns farm data. Founded in 2012 and headquartered at the Texas Tech Innovation Hub, GiSC now has members across 41 states and all major commodities, and is the only producer-owned organization in the world focused solely on ag technology and data.

He has testified before the House Agriculture Committee on farm data policy, serves on the board of Ag Data Transparent, and has been named by PrecisionAg among the ten most intriguing people shaping precision agriculture in the United States. Billy will host the bus Monday morning, setting the stage for the week's conversations about data, trust, and the farmer's stake in both.

Andy Timmons

Bus Host & Emcee, Monday Afternoon · Lost Draw Vineyards, Brownfield, Texas

A Texas Tech agronomy graduate, Andy Timmons founded Lost Draw Vineyards in Brownfield, Texas, and co-founded Lost Draw Cellars in Fredericksburg — helping establish the Texas High Plains as one of the premier wine regions in the country. He will host the bus for the Monday afternoon portion of the tour.

Michael Ratke

Bus Host & Emcee, Tuesday · Southwest Ag Services & The Pumpkin Patch · Farwell, Texas

Michael is the owner and operator of Southwest Ag Services and The Pumpkin Patch in Farwell, Texas — the second largest decorative pumpkin shipper in the Southern United States. A SWOSU graduate with an MBA in Economics, he brings a unique perspective at the intersection of specialty crops, agronomy, and agricultural retail.

The Binghams

Meadow, Texas — Opening Farm Stop

Pioneers of Texas organic agriculture, the Binghams have been certifying 2,000 acres since 1991 under Bingham Family Vineyards. Kyle Bingham now leads day-to-day operations, carrying forward the family's multi-decade commitment to sustainable farming practices on the Texas High Plains.

Sam Nesbitt

Market President, First United Bank · Brownfield, Texas

Sam Nesbitt brings 15 years of agricultural and commercial banking experience. A graduate of the TALL XVII program, he speaks on the financial side of conservation agriculture, bridging the gap between regenerative practices and rural lending.

Terry Soil and Water Conservation District

208 West Broadway · Brownfield, Texas

Governed by an elected board of local producers, the Terry SWCD runs educational programs including Farm Tour for Kidz and Women in Ag, connecting the next generation with conservation principles rooted in the Texas High Plains tradition.

South Plains Underground Water Conservation District

Brownfield, Texas

The South Plains UWCD manages the groundwater of Terry County and the Ogallala Aquifer. The district delivers the Southern Ogallala Conservation and Outreach Program (SOCOP), working to protect and conserve one of the nation's most critical water resources.

Barry Evans

Kress, Texas

Barry Evans farms 4,500 acres of cotton and grain in Kress, Texas. He converted to no-till farming in 1996 and has not looked back. Named Field to Market's 2021 Farmer of the Year, Barry is a recognized leader in soil health and conservation agriculture on the Texas High Plains.

Jeremy and Sarah Brown

Broadview Agriculture · Dawson County, Texas

A fourth-generation family farm, Broadview Agriculture is Regenified-certified and operated by Jeremy and Sarah Brown in Dawson County. Their operation serves as a working model for integrating regenerative principles into large-scale row cropping systems.

Dean and Lacy Vardeman

Vardeman Farms · Slaton, Texas

Lacy Cotter-Vardeman is a leading voice in regenerative agriculture and founder of SARA (Sustainable Agriculture Research Alliance). The Vardemans farm 5,000 acres of cotton in Slaton, Texas, and are opening their home for a roundtable dinner with local farmers on Tuesday evening.

Dr. Todd Baughman

Center Director, Texas A&M AgriLife Research and Extension Center · Lubbock, Texas

Dr. Baughman is a weed scientist and agronomist with a Ph.D. from Mississippi State University. Previously at Oklahoma State University, he now directs one of the most impactful agricultural research stations on the Southern High Plains.

Dr. Katie Lewis

Soil Chemistry & Fertility Scientist, Texas A&M AgriLife Research · Lubbock, Texas

A professor jointly appointed at Texas A&M University and Texas Tech University, Dr. Lewis specializes in nutrient management, resilient cropping systems, and soil health. She is the recipient of the TCEQ Environmental Excellence Award (2026).

Dr. Ethan Triplett

Research and Carbon Monitoring Coordinator, National Sorghum Producers · Lubbock, Texas

A crop scientist with a Ph.D. from Texas Tech University, Dr. Triplett works at the cutting edge of sorghum research and carbon monitoring, helping connect producers with emerging soil carbon markets.

Oklahoma

Susan Bergen

Bergen Enterprises · Sulphur, Oklahoma

Susan Bergen operates Bergen Enterprises cattle operations in Sulphur, Oklahoma. She has been practicing restorative grazing since 2012 and was named 2023 Landowner Conservationist of the Year. She and her team will host the group for the iconic Susan Bergen Horse Barn Dinner on Wednesday evening.

Dr. Kent Martin

Oklahoma Host · Professor of Agronomy, Northwestern Oklahoma State University

A sixth-generation Oklahoma farmer and rancher, Dr. Martin is a professor of agronomy at Northwestern Oklahoma State University and a member of the NASA Acres Farm Innovation Ambassador Team (FIAT). He also operates Martin Ag Consulting, bringing deep local knowledge of Oklahoma's diverse agricultural landscape to the tour.

Kansas

Ray and Ryan Flickner

Flickner Innovation Farms · Moundridge, Kansas

A fifth and sixth generation family farm dating to the 1870s, the Flickners started their Innovation Farm in 2018 on 850+ acres. Ray and Ryan serve on the FIAT steering committee and have received numerous awards for conservation and innovation in agriculture — including the Kansas Water Resources Natural Resources award, the Kansas Bankers Association Water Conservation Award, and an invitation to testify before Congress. The Flickners will host the Space4Ag Tour's Kansas farm day.

Susan Metzger

Director of Strategic Interdisciplinary Program Development, Kansas State University

Susan Metzger is director of strategic interdisciplinary program development at Kansas State University, where she leads the Kansas Water Institute and the Kansas Center for Agricultural Resources and the Environment. She collaborates with university leadership and external partners to advance integrated agricultural programs, bringing deep expertise in water policy, natural resource management, and stakeholder engagement built over 25 years as a scientist and leader in water resource planning.

Before K-State, Susan was deputy secretary of the Kansas Department of Agriculture, serving the farmers, ranchers and agribusinesses of Kansas, and spent 11 years at the Kansas Water Office as chief of planning and policy, where she was instrumental in developing the Long-Term Vision for the Future of Water Supply in Kansas. She earned a bachelor's degree in biological sciences from the University of Mary Washington, a master's in biological sciences from Old Dominion University, and a doctorate in leadership communications from K-State.
